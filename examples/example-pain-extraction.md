---

## Pain Point 1: Support failures stop the business

**Pain**
When something breaks, support tickets sit unanswered for days while the
prospect's work grinds to a halt.

**Evidence**
> "When something breaks with our current vendor, we open a ticket and just wait.
> Sometimes days. And we're a small team, so when the system goes down, our work
> stops."

**Why it matters**
For a small team, downtime isn't an inconvenience — it's lost revenue. The
prospect already lost a customer to a failure that went unnoticed, so slow
support translates directly into churn.

---

## Pain Point 2: Accidental ownership of a system they don't understand

**Pain**
The prospect has become the de facto owner of a critical system despite it not
being their role or area of expertise.

**Evidence**
> "I've basically become the go-to person for this by accident, which is strange
> because it's not even my job. I don't fully understand what's happening under
> the hood."

**Why it matters**
This is both an operational risk (a non-expert running something critical) and a
personal frustration. It signals the prospect wants to *offload* the burden, not
just get a cheaper tool.

---

## Pain Point 3: Unpredictable pricing makes planning impossible

**Pain**
Surprise fees cause the monthly bill to fluctuate, making it impossible to budget.

**Evidence**
> "I never know what our bill's going to be. It jumps around and there's always
> some fee I didn't expect. I can't plan for it."

**Why it matters**
Budget unpredictability erodes trust in the vendor and makes the relationship feel
adversarial rather than a partnership. Predictable pricing would be a strong
differentiator.
